You've seen it before: a role that looks perfect, a clean application, and then silence. Weeks pass. The posting is still there, still accepting applications — but nobody is reading them. That posting may be a ghost job.

## What is a ghost job?

A ghost job is a job posting that is no longer attached to an active hiring process. The posting exists — it sits live on a board or a career page — but the role behind it is already filled, frozen, or was never genuinely open in the first place. The company isn't hiring for it, and applications sent to it go nowhere.

Keep one thing in mind: a ghost job is not a scam. Scams are a separate problem involving fake companies and fake recruiters. A ghost job usually comes from a real employer with real hiring needs — just not for this posting, right now. The posting has outlived its purpose, and nobody has taken it down.

## Why do ghost jobs exist?

Most ghost jobs aren't created on purpose. They're leftovers of normal hiring processes:

- **The role was filled and nobody removed the posting.** By far the most common case. An offer was made, someone accepted, and deleting the posting wasn't assigned to anyone.
- **The posting is a pipeline.** Some teams keep postings open to collect resumes for future openings even when nothing is open today. The posting is "evergreen," and every application goes into a pool.
- **Hiring was frozen.** Budget or headcount was pulled mid-process. Pausing the posting is manual work, so it stays live.
- **The role is filled but not yet started.** The company hired someone who starts in two months, and it keeps the posting up as insurance in case the offer falls through.
- **It's a placeholder.** The team plans to hire eventually and wants the posting ready so it doesn't have to restart from zero.

None of this is sinister. But for you, the applicant, the result is the same: time and attention spent on an application that may never be read.

## How common are ghost jobs?

The honest answer is that nobody knows the true number for the entire labor market, and anyone who quotes you a precise global figure is guessing. What we can do is measure what we actually observe. HireClarity Data tracks roughly 8,000 live postings across about 90 companies, checking each one daily on ATS-hosted boards like Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, and Workable, plus public company career pages. LinkedIn and Indeed restrict automated access, so they are not part of our sample — and our coverage is always labeled as exactly that: our observed sample.

From that observed sample we can measure how long postings stay listed, how often they are taken down and relisted, and how many show the patterns of a ghost. Those aggregate numbers are published every month in a free public report — the August 2026 edition, covering the first full month of tracking, is live at https://hireclarity-data.vercel.app/reports/2026-08. Individual company scores stay behind the subscription, but the market-level picture is public.

The report also shows which industries are hiring the most, the most common job titles, and the share of postings that ask for a degree or five-plus years of experience — all drawn from the same observed sample. Trend direction is marked "n/a" until a second month of history exists; we'd rather label a blank honestly than invent a number.

The result is a confidence score with plain-language reasons you can read.

The score is a signal, not a verdict. A high score doesn't prove your application will be ignored — sometimes a stale-looking posting is actively hiring. It means the posting shows the patterns we associate with postings that aren't attached to live hiring. Use it to prioritize your time, not to rule things out.

One thing the score deliberately doesn't do is judge the company. It only uses public observations of the posting itself — how long it has been listed and how it behaves over time. We can't see whether anyone is reading applications, and we don't pretend to. The score answers one narrow question: does this posting look like it's attached to live hiring?

## What you can do today

Before you spend an hour tailoring a resume, a few quick checks cost almost nothing:

1. Look at the posting date if the board shows one. Postings listed for months deserve extra scrutiny.
2. Check the company's own career page. If the role isn't there, that's a signal.
3. Watch for relist patterns — a posting that vanishes and reappears is often being recycled.
4. Read the reasons behind the score, not just the number.
