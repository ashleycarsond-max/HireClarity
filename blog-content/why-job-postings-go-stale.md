A job posting goes stale when it stops reflecting reality. The company no longer wants what the posting describes, or no longer wants it on the timeline the posting implies — but the posting stays up, unchanged, as if nothing happened. Stale postings are the reason "job postings not removed" shows up in every job-seeker forum, and they're worth understanding because they cost both sides of the table.

## What "stale" means

Stale isn't the same as ghost, though the two overlap. A ghost job was never attached to live hiring, or stopped being attached a long time ago. A stale posting may have been perfectly real six months ago — a real team, a real budget, a real plan to hire. Then something changed, and the posting was never updated or removed. The posting is a museum piece: accurate once, irrelevant now.

## How postings go stale

It's rarely one dramatic event. Usually it's a quiet process:

- **The role was filled.** The single most common path. Once the candidate accepts, there's no process left to keep the posting honest.
- **The hiring owner left.** The recruiter or manager who owned the role moved on, and the posting lost its advocate. Nobody else knows it exists, let alone whether it should be updated.
- **The org changed.** The team was reorganized, the role was split, or the headcount was repurposed. The posting describes a job that no longer exists in that form.
- **The budget vanished.** Freezes are often announced verbally and never translated into "take the posting down." It sits there waiting for money that isn't coming.
- **The ATS runs on autopilot.** Many applicant tracking systems republish or extend postings by default. A posting can stay live without a single human decision — the software keeps the candle burning on its own.

Notice what these have in common: no one owns the posting's end of life. Hiring teams plan the start of a posting — the job description, the budget, the launch — but almost nobody plans its removal.

## What staleness signals

For job seekers, a stale posting is a time trap. It accepts applications, it looks current, and it burns the most valuable thing you have: attention. Learning to read staleness is a practical skill.

For companies, stale postings are a quiet leak. Candidates who apply and hear nothing form an impression, and that impression compounds across every stale posting on the careers page. Investors and candidates increasingly look at hiring behavior as a health signal — a board full of stale postings reads as disorganization even when the company is thriving. Framed as reputation protection: keeping postings honest is one of the cheapest ways to look well-run.

The fix is unglamorous and cheap: decide which postings are real, update or remove the rest, and schedule the review so it happens more than once a year. Companies that do this consistently read as organized — and organization is a signal in both directions, to candidates and to investors.

## What the data shows

We can quantify staleness because we watch postings continuously. HireClarity Data tracks roughly 8,000 live postings across about 90 companies, re-reviewing each one every few hours on ATS-hosted boards like Greenhouse, Ashby, and Lever, plus company career pages. From that observed sample we measure how long postings sit untouched, how often they're taken down and relisted, and how boards compare. LinkedIn and Indeed restrict automated access, so they aren't in our sample — the coverage is always labeled as our observed sample.

The limits are real and stated: the sample covers ATS-hosted boards and company career pages, not every platform, and it has been running since August 2026 — enough for a first month of numbers, not yet for trends. That's why trend direction is honestly marked "n/a" in the report for now. The aggregate numbers are public: the monthly report at https://hireclarity-data.vercel.app/reports/2026-08 shows observed listing durations, relist activity, score distributions, the industries hiring the most, the most common titles, and the share of postings requiring degrees or five-plus years of experience. We'd rather label a blank than invent a number.

## How to handle a stale posting

- **Check the age and the company's other postings.** One old posting among fresh ones is likely an oversight; an entire board that hasn't changed in months is a different story.
- **Look for relists.** A posting that disappears and comes back is being actively managed — and often recycled.
- **Use the free check tool.** Paste the posting URL and get a confidence score with reasons grounded in what we've observed about that specific posting.
- **Apply anyway if it's a great fit.** Staleness is a signal, not a ban. The downside of applying to a stale posting is small; the upside of the rare genuinely slow process is real. Just don't let it be your only application.

And remember that staleness is about the posting, not the company. One stale listing can sit next to a great employer; the signal is worth folding into your decision, not treating as a verdict on the whole organization.

For the fuller picture of why postings stay listed for months in the first place, read why do job postings stay up so long: /blog/why-do-job-postings-stay-up-so-long.

Stale postings won't disappear on their own — boards don't expire them and companies forget them. The fix is the same as it is for ghost jobs: visibility. If everyone can see which postings are alive, the ones that aren't stop costing everyone time.
